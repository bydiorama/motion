# Changelog

## Unreleased

- `parallax` on a layer: its depth against the scene's camera (1 travels with the scene, above 1 nearer and faster, 0 holds still), or `{ depth, home }` with the moment it sits where it is placed (the scene's end by default). A part of a picture can travel ahead of the camera and land in place without a counter-track worked out by hand. It follows the camera's x and y travel, on an artboard in design px.
- Moves `wipe` and `slide` take an `angle` (degrees; 0 rightwards, 90 downwards). An angled `wipe` cuts the layer square to its direction and, with `amount`, carries it that many px along it, so a slanted band unrolls from one end. `cutPolygon()` is exported.
- `motion beats <audio|film>`: a soundtrack's tempo (to a hundredth of a bpm), its downbeats, and the stretches of `--bars` bars that loop best, scored by how alike the music is either side of the seam. For a film, whether its `tempo`, `audio.from` and loop agree with the music, and the `from` that puts its start on the beat. ffmpeg decodes the file; the analysis (`analyseBeats`, `loopWindows`, `seams`) is pure.
- `motion import <design.json>`: a scene that builds a layout from its exported parts. A design manifest lists the frame's size and background and its parts bottom to top, each with its box and an image or a fill. The scene puts them on the frame's `artboard` in its px, keeps their stacking, and times them in reading order a `--step` apart: rules wipe across, type rises into its line (`mask-up`), pictures arrive last (`slot-up`). `sceneFromDesign` does the same from a script.
- Artboards: a scene's `artboard` (`width`, `height`, `fit`: `cover`, `contain`, `width` or `height`, `align`) is the frame a design was drawn on. Layers placed with `"unit": "px"` are laid out on it in the design's own pixels, with their sizes, tracks and move amounts, and the board is scaled onto the stage; a layer placed in % stays on the stage and keeps its place in the stacking order. Without an artboard, px are stage px. `artboardFit()` is exported. `staggerFrom` builds px layers on an `artboard` too.
- Soundtracks: a film's `audio` (`src`, `from`, `gain`, `fade_in`, `fade_out`) plays the file from `from` seconds at film time 0. The studio and the page player follow the playhead with it; `render` cuts it to the film and lays it under the video (AAC), de-clicked at both ends. With a soundtrack the music sets the tempo, so `beat-off-frame` is not warned. The preview server serves audio files.
- Fix: `wipe-left` and `wipe-right` ran the wrong way; like `wipe-up` and `wipe-down`, their edge now travels in the named direction, in and out.
- Fix: on a scene whose camera moves, a placed layer is no longer capped at the stage width, so a rule or a picture laid across the canvas keeps its size and its wipes clip at its own edges.
- Scenes are laid end to end in exact time and only their edges are snapped to frames, so on a beat that falls between frames the cuts stay within half a frame of their beats instead of drifting a frame further with every scene.
- Move `slot-up`: a layer rises `amount` px (48 by default) into its own box, cut off at the box's bottom edge, with no fade, on `cubic.out`: a framed picture settling into a slot that was already there. `mask-up` travels the layer's whole height.
- `fx/pan-across`: the camera travels along a picture wider (or taller) than its frame, filling it the other way, on `sine.inOut`. It reports its fastest speed, so `pace` can warn before it strobes.
- Primitive contract: optional `speed(props, params, ctx)`, the fastest a layer's picture crosses the stage in px a second. `pace` warns `judder` when a frame-to-frame step is over a fortieth of the stage's shorter side.
- `fx/slow-push`: `focus_x` and `focus_y`, the point of the picture the crop is taken around and the push grows from, so a subject off centre stays in frame.
- `transition/strip-wipe`: `mode: cover` closes the rows over the old scene, holds the frame in colour on the cut, then opens on the next; `alternate` runs every other row the other way.
- Pace: a plain cut lands on any beat; only a hand-over must land on a bar line, so a run of quick cuts can keep the pulse. Drift (`fx/slow-push`, `fx/pan-across`, anything whose cues are empty) is counted apart: it keeps a film from reading as `stalled`, never makes it `restless`, and shows as `~` in the rhythm chart. New `even-cuts`: five or more shots in a row of one length.
- CRAFT: "Cut, mostly" (one picture to a frame, long and short shots in turn, transitions only at the turns), choosing the shot by the picture, pan speed, and "From identity to motion", a checklist for deriving the grid, the signature transition, the mark's build, the colours and the curves from an identity.
- `transition/strip-wipe`: the next scene arrives strip by strip across the rows of a grid, each row led by a field of colour from the palette (`band-1` … `band-6`, or a surface), one row a step after the last, so the cut reads as a staircase. `rows`, `order` (down, up, centre), `stagger`, `lead` and `direction`.
- `scene/post-carousel` reports each turn's share of the frame, so a carousel of small posters is not weighed as a full-frame change. `slideSize()` is exported.
- Pace counts only the changes that can be seen: from the middle of the hand-over in to the middle of the one out. A wall cut away mid-round no longer reads as busier than it is.
- Pace measures density as how much of the frame changes a second: a change counts by its share of the frame (`share` on a cue; `scene/bento-grid` reports each tile's). A wall swapping one tile at a time no longer reads as several times busier than a scene of whole pictures. The pace table gains a `frame/s` column.
- `--no-tune` now reaches the page: `stills`, `check` and `render` show the film as written, not only `validate` and `pace`.
- Primitives can declare `cycles`: a layer that runs in rounds (`scene/bento-grid`) may be ended by its scene on any rest, without a `cut-off` warning. Ending it in the middle of a change is reported as `cut-mid-change`.
- Rhythm. A film can set `"tempo": {"bpm": 120, "bar": 4}`, and then write any time in beats or bars: `"2b"`, `"0.5b"`, `"1bar"`, in `at`, in offsets (`"title.end + 1b"`), in scene `length` and `hold`, in move and hand-over durations, in params measured in seconds and in track keys. Change the bpm and the whole film retimes in proportion. `validate` warns when a beat is not a whole number of frames and names tempos that are; a beat without a tempo is an error (`needs-tempo`).
- `motion pace <film>`: the film's rhythm, measured. Per scene, its length, hand-overs in and out, time alone, share in motion, changes and rests; the lengths of every hand-over, change, enter and exit; and a chart of the film beat by beat (or quarter second). It warns when a hand-over outweighs the scenes it joins (`handover-long`: over a third of the shorter; `handover-crowds`: a scene alone for less than twice its hand-over; `handover-vs-picture`: longer than half of one picture's time), when like moves take different times (`uneven-handovers`, `uneven-changes`, `uneven-rests`), when one scene changes far more often than another (`uneven-density`), when pictures rest under 1 s (`short-rest`), when the film moves over 60% or under 20% of the time (`restless`, `stalled`), and with a tempo, when a cut misses a bar line, a move the half-beat, or a loop whole bars (`off-grid`, `off-beat`, `loop-bars`). `validate` reports the same warnings. Pure, like `validate`: no browser.
- Primitive contract: optional `cues(props, params, ctx)`, the changes inside a primitive's own run (`{ at, length, lane }`), so the pace check can read a wall, a carousel or a strip cycle change by change. `scene/strip-cycle`, `scene/bento-grid` and `scene/post-carousel` report theirs; footage and drift (`el/video`, `el/frames`, `fx/slow-push`) report none.
- `docs/CRAFT.md`: a Rhythm section (tempo and whole-frame beats, bars and phrases, one length per kind of move, a ladder in beats, even density, seams not sections, a third in motion, time to see a picture, story before rhythm). Sections after it are renumbered. The `motion-film` skill plans the rhythm in bars before writing layers, writes times in beats, and runs `pace` before looking at frames.
- `scene/strip-cycle`: `shift` is now the whole change, first strip leaving to last landing, so a change lasts exactly what the film says; the stagger is spread inside it. All strips run one way by default (`direction: left`: the way they run, entering from the right, as for `band-sweep`), stacking into a staircase of colour; `alternate` and `weave` remain for identities built on cuts.
- `scene/post-carousel`: bare posters (`chrome: none`) take the stage's `--m-card-radius` and `--m-card-shadow` when it sets them, so an identity with square corners and no depth keeps them.
- `transition/band-sweep`: 1 s by default (two beats at 120 bpm), from 0.5 s. With `angle: 0` the bands lie flat and exactly their share of the frame, so they meet on the rows of a grid instead of overlapping; the entry says when alternating tilts read as a zig-zag.
- `scene/strip-cycle`: a frame ruled into horizontal strips; pictures take turns filling it and each change travels strip by strip, a field of colour running ahead of the next picture, neighbouring strips from opposite sides (`direction: weave` also turns the pattern each change). The rules stay drawn over the pictures. `empty` begins and/or ends on the bare ruled ground, and `ground_hold` sets how long that ground holds, so one layer can open from a grid and close back onto it for a seamless loop. `fills`, `rule`, `rows`, `hold`, `shift`, `stagger`, `order`, `lead`, `drag`, `push`, `rule_width`, `rule_opacity`, `edges`.
- `transition/band-sweep`: bands of colour stream across the frame at slightly different tilts, swell until together they cover it, cut underneath, then run on out narrowing. Colours come from `--m-band-1` … `--m-band-6` (set them in `theme.vars` with an identity's palette), falling back to the accent, inverse and surfaces. `direction`, `bands`, `width`, `angle`, `lag`; calm preset.
- `docs/CRAFT.md`: band-sweep in the transitions table; two loop rules (close on the ground you opened on; keep the rest at the seam short).
- `theme.ui`: a film can wear a bundled bydiorama/ui theme (`zero`, Diorama's own; `graphite`; `hockney`). The stage gets that theme's `--ui-*` tokens and the Aspekta face (embedded, so stills and renders match the studio), scoped to the stage, in the film's `scheme`. `scripts/ui-theme.ts` writes the themes to `src/dom/ui-themes.css.ts`. `bento-reel` and `post-carousel` are restyled in Zero with role colours only (no hex), and the wizard's thumbnails wear Graphite, like the page around them.
- `scene/bento-grid`: tiles of different sizes on one grid, laid out by named areas (`"a b b c / d b b e"`), each cycling its own items on its own clock. Every tile swaps once a period, a phase (`offset`) apart in reading order; a tile with one item holds. Swap moves per tile: `slide-up`, `slide-left`, `flip`, `stack` and `fade` (`swap: mixed` gives neighbours different ones), always from one item straight to the next. Items are markup or picture paths; pictures drift in their tile. With `stagger` 0 and counts that divide the largest, a scene as long as the layer ends on its first frame. Demo: `films/bento-reel.film.mjs`.
- `scene/post-carousel`: slides shown one at a time, as a social post (`chrome: post`: header with avatar and handle, footer with actions and dots that follow the front slide) or as bare posters (`chrome: none`). The slides stand side by side in one world; the neighbours wait turned away (`angle`), hinged on the edge nearest the frame, and each step the row turns. `ratio`, `hold`, `turn`, and `loop` to turn on from the last slide to the first. Demo: `films/post-carousel.film.mjs`.
- Film builder: `iso`, isometric drawing for film scripts. The view is the one `rig/layer-stack` draws with `lay` 54.7356, `turn` -45 and `lens` 0, so flat SVG drawn with it lines up with the rig exactly. `iso.point` and `iso.unproject` map between the world and the stage, `iso.matrix` lays a flat drawing onto the floor or an upright face, `iso.path` and `iso.quad` draw through world points, `iso.lattice` draws the floor as dots. Because the view is parallel, a move in the world is the same move on stage anywhere, so isometric pieces animate with ordinary x and y tracks.
- `rig/layer-stack`: named planes lying flat, one above the other, seen through a long lens, each with a label that faces the camera. They close onto a base that never moves, lowest first, each dissolving into what it lands on; the top face then takes the accent, the stack's `name` appears and one ring leaves the base. `order` is `close`, `open` or `open-close`; `faces` gives each plane a picture; `lay`, `turn` and `spin` set the planes' own angle under the shared camera. `docs/CRAFT.md` gains the rule it follows (many become one by landing, not by fading). `lens` sets the length of the lens (0 is parallel, for a true isometric drawing with `lay: 54.7356`, `turn: -45`), and `look: wire` draws outlined planes with dotted corner guides and numbered mono labels.
- The stage's mono face reads `--ui-font-mono` when a theme sets it.
- `docs/CRAFT.md`: a film has an anchor, and the best anchor is the world itself (a fixed floor and a slot marked from the first frame), not chrome repeated over cuts.
- `el/video`: footage as one layer instead of one image layer per frame. `src`, `from`, `to`, `rate`, `loop`, `crop` ([x, y, w, h] in % of the source), `width`, `height`, `radius`. `render`, `check` and `stills` pull the frames each layer shows with ffmpeg (cached in `out/<film>/frames/`), so renders stay frame-exact; the studio plays a `<video>`. `continues: "scene.layer"` opens a shot on the frame after another layer's last one, so a film can loop through footage without a seam. `window.seek` now returns a promise that resolves once footage has loaded.
- Scene `follow`: the camera holds the tip of a drawing line (`fx/path-draw`) at a point on stage, with `lag` and `blend`, while scale and rotate tracks still zoom and roll. `fx/path-draw` gains an `ease` param.
- `scene/grid-wall` is drawable: masonry (each picture under the shortest column; equal proportions make a regular grid) that opens nearest a focus point, then drifts. `staggerByDistance` orders any set of positions from a point.
- Film builder (`@bydiorama/motion/build`: `film`, `scene`, `layer`, `repeat`, `grid`, `staggerFrom`, `sequence`) and `motion make script.mjs`, which checks the returned film and writes the JSON. See `examples/builder.film.mjs`.
- Calm transitions: the `transitions` token (`standard` or `calm`) swaps every transition's defaults for a quiet preset, e.g. `camera-travel` pull 0.04, tilt 5, gap 0. The calm feel and `style/editorial-type` set it.
- `motion capture <url> --sections`: a screenshot per section at 2×, with cookie banners and fixed headers hidden. `--browser <path>` (or `MOTION_CHROMIUM`) runs every drawing command with an installed Chromium-based browser such as Brave.
- `check --loop` (and films with `"loop": true`) measures the seam from last frame to first next to a normal frame-to-frame step. In scenes whose camera moves, layers are reported only when the camera never brings them into view (`never-in-view`).
- Fixes: `mask-up`/`mask-down` no longer cut descenders, accents or old-style figures (the mask leaves `--m-mask-pad`, 0.3em, above and below the line, and lets go without a jump). `el/image` puts its radius on the layer too, so shadows and outlines follow the corners, and gains `height`, `frame` and `bevel`. Placed layers are as wide as their text up to `style.width` (or the room their anchor leaves), instead of wrapping at the space right of their left edge. `bad-id` says which rule an id broke and offers a fix; `never-seen` explains frame snapping and names the last frame a layer can start on.
- Primitive contract: optional `check` (cross-field validation), `pending` (async loads) and `tip` (a point a camera can follow); transitions can carry a `calm` preset.
- `el/frames` plays a run backwards when `last` is before `first`.
- A placed layer is no longer capped to the room its anchor leaves on stage when its scene's camera moves: on a canvas, layers lie beyond the stage on purpose, and the cap clipped pictures near the edge and emptied those past it.
- `check`'s loop seam is held against the largest frame-to-frame step near both ends, over a fifth of a second each, instead of the median of five: footage slower than the film (24 fps in 60) holds on most frames, so its median step is nearly nothing and a sound seam was reported as a jump.
- `transition/fly-past`: the outgoing scene's ground now dissolves over the next scene, which is whole beneath it from the first frame. Before, the ground went in one frame while the next scene faded in, so the gaps between the layers dipped through the stage's own colour.
- Footage, pages and stills, from the craft of a website loop built on film footage. `el/frames` plays a numbered run of stills as footage (beside `el/video`, which plays a video file; use frames for a recorded scroll or stills already cut from a film): the frame on screen is a pure function of time (first frame, last frame, source fps, rate, a delay held on the first frame), so footage scrubs, repeats and renders exactly, and a shot is one layer instead of one image layer per frame. `ui/page-scroll` scrolls a page (one capture, or its sections stacked) inside a window, leg by leg with rests at chosen stops, blurred along the scroll by its speed. `fx/slow-push` holds a picture in a fixed frame and drifts it: push in, pull out or pan, never showing an edge.
- `ui/page-scroll` shares its travelling time between legs by the square root of their length, so a long leg is not a flash beside a short one.
- `motion check` no longer compares the two draws of a frame by hash. It compares the page's state (every element's box, opacity, visibility, filter, clip and transform), which must match exactly, and the pixels with room: up to 2 levels in at most 0.1% of the pixels passes, more is a `raster-differs` warning when the state is identical (the browser rasterising the same state differently), and an error only when whole areas differ. Messages say how much differs.
- `docs/CRAFT.md`: rules for a headline over a montage (it arrives whole), for scrolling a page, for stills in a hold, and for a route of stations (an even, unhurried pace), as corrected in review. `narrative/website-launch` lists the new moves.
- Depth and camera, from the craft of our 2026 website reel. `docs/CRAFT.md` gains a section on it (one world per hand-over, blur along the path sized by speed, stacking lifts, cross-fades over one-frame switches, same size on screen, shared rhythm, opening from black), and rules for many equal steps (`sine.inOut`) and for the first frame as a thumbnail.
- Motion blur: any layer can set `"motionBlur": true` (or a shutter, 0–1). The engine measures the layer's speed from its poses half a frame either side and smears it along its path only, with an SVG Gaussian sized by speed; it is gone when the layer stops. `transition/push` now blurs along the push only.
- 4 transitions: `transition/camera-travel` (both scenes in one world; the camera pulls back, leans and travels), `transition/whip-pan` (anticipation, then a smear along the pan, braking on the next scene), `transition/signal-wipe` (a line of accent light leaves the next scene behind it), `transition/fly-past` (a scene's layers fly out past the camera, front first, revealing the next beneath).
- 4 primitives: `type/fill-text` (the line is there from frame 0, dim, and fills word by word), `texture/signal-field` (static that settles into a calm grid from the centre out, then pulses), `rig/browser-window` (a page turns into perspective and lifts one of its parts, shadow growing with height), `fx/pin-drop` (markers land, ripple in one phase and raise their labels on stems).
- Camera controls for the 3D rigs: a shared camera (`src/primitives/rig/camera.ts`) with `path` (`in`, `out`, `in-out`, `hold`), `tilt` and `pitch` on one studio pad, `roll`, `zoom` (a true distance), `pan_x` and `pan_y` on a second pad (where it looks and turns about), `move`, `hold`, `ease` and `drift`. `rig/browser-window` uses it (its `tilt`, `pitch`, `move` and `drift` keep their names) and gains `lift_at`.
- `rig/tab-stack`: tabs stacked as stairs in depth; the open state slides through them on a sine, sharing one tab's height between neighbours, each tab cross-fading between its closed and open look on one title row; the tab that ends open pops forward. Uses the shared camera.
- Studio: choice and ease params get a dropdown (they had no dial before), so a camera path or a transition's direction can be picked in the inspector.
- `transition/dive-in` gains `pace: glide`: one eased push that arrives at rest, blurring only while it moves, for match cuts.
- `narrative/website-launch`: a new site as one journey, each page handing over to the next. `style/dark-showcase` and `style/product-ui` use the new moves.
- Added `films/signal-reel.film.json` (4:5, 60 fps), which exercises all of the above.
- Runtime fix: finished hand-overs are now cleared before the frame's hand-overs are drawn. A scene that was the incoming side of one transition and the outgoing side of the next lost the second transition's styles when frames were drawn out of order.
- Primitives get the film's `fps` in their context (`transition/push` read the 30 fps default token, so its blur was wrong at 60 fps).
- CLI: `MOTION_CHROMIUM` points stills, check and render at a Chromium binary; the preview page declares an empty icon, so `check` no longer reports the browser's favicon request as an error.
- Added `docs/CRAFT.md`: the numbers behind designed motion — easing roles, spring presets, a duration scale for video, stagger and order, text reveals, composition and safe areas, transitions, logo-reveal patterns, end cards, data staging and loops. The `motion-film` skill reads it.
- Eases: `quad`, `quart`, `quint` and `circ` (in, out, in-out), `back.in`, `back.inOut`, `elastic.out`, and five expressive curves: `expressive.out`, `expressive.in`, `expressive.inOut`, `snap.out`, `anticipate.in`.
- Moves: `zoom`, `pop`, `focus`, `mask-up`, `mask-down`, `wipe-up`, `wipe-down`, `wipe-left`, `wipe-right`, `swing`. Masks and wipes uncover without fading; `pop` and `swing` bring their own spring when no ease is given. Tracks gain `rotateX`, `rotateY` and `skew`.
- Props can be lists of numbers (`number[]`), for charts.
- 18 primitives drawn by the runtime: `type/mask-reveal`, `type/char-cascade`, `type/block-reveal`, `type/word-roller`, `type/tracking-in`, `type/shimmer`, `type/echo`, `type/scribble-highlight`, `fx/odometer`, `fx/path-draw`, `fx/shape-morph`, `fx/grid-ripple`, `scene/logo-reveal`, `scene/bar-chart`, `scene/line-chart`, `texture/gradient-drift`, `texture/grain`, `ui/cursor-click`. Shared helpers split text into masked lines, words and letters, stagger from the start, end, centre, edges or at random (seeded, by distance on grids), and resample shapes for morphing.
- 7 transitions drawn by the runtime: `transition/push`, `transition/iris`, `transition/cover-reveal`, `transition/dive-in`, `transition/curtain`, `transition/stack-over`, `transition/slant-wipe`.
- Library: entries for the new primitives (most now `specified`), with params matching the runtime; `style/kinetic-poster` developed; styles and narratives use the new moves, drawable ones first so briefs and animatics can show them.
- Added `films/craft-reel.film.json`, a reel that exercises every new move.
- Runtime: scenes off screen are now `display: none` as well as hidden, because blend modes and SVG filters still painted through `visibility: hidden` and made frames depend on what was drawn before. Transitions hand over with scene opacity, never visibility. `check` no longer reports layers off stage while their scene is in a transition, or full-bleed textures that bleed by design. The primitive contract now says: children shown again use visibility `inherit`, never `visible`.
- Added `motion wizard`: a one-question-per-screen page that turns a video type, feel, message, moves, length and formats into a brief to paste into Claude or ChatGPT. `--out` writes it as a single HTML file.

- Added the `motion-film` agent skill, with a field-by-field film reference and fixes for common errors.

- Studio: the scene list shows a still of each scene, drawn by the runtime, with a ring on the current one (no scene numbers; the still's radius is concentric with its card). The inspector is a flat column; DialKit's stacked folder borders are replaced by space between groups. On phones the transport controls are centred.
